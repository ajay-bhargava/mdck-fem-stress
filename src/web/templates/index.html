How does a sheet
of cells carry force?

A layer of cells is more than a collection of living parts. Together, they form a material that moves, pulls, and carries stress.

Here, MDCK cells, a laboratory model of kidney epithelial tissue, grow on a soft gel. Their pulls deform the gel. Traction-force microscopy uses that deformation to estimate the forces beneath the sheet.

Images alone cannot tell us how those forces are carried inside the tissue. A finite element model combines the measured loads with explicit mechanical assumptions to predict stress. Comparing stress with cell motion can eventually help us test how living sheets behave as materials.
